## 1. The structural difference, and the thing it turns out not to be

I expected Article 14 of Directive (EU) 2019/790 to reach France through a transposing
provision and Turkey not at all. Half of that is wrong. **France is reported among the
member states that declined to transpose Article 14 separately**, on the view that its
existing law already produces the result. **[fetched: Europeana PRO / Common European Data
Space survey "Article 14 and the public domain — the state of play across Europe", reached
through search; the page body would not fetch, so treat the list as a pointer.]** D-005
says Article 14 "reaches France through transposition." There is no separate French
provision to read.

For `black-fuji` (CC BY 3.0, flat Hokusai print) and `vahine-no-te-tiare` (CC BY-SA 4.0,
flat Gauguin canvas): **France** asks the ordinary originality question with no Article 14
text of its own to point at **[general]**. **Turkey** has no analogue, but its own two-tier
apparatus: FSEK treats photographs of aesthetic character as works, while **Article 84
routes photographs that are not works through property and unfair-competition rules rather
than copyright** — protection without the word infringement. **[fetched: search summaries
only; I did not fetch the statutory text and am unsure of the article's exact scope.]**

Same question, two machines, **no verified answer in either.** But notice what an answer
changes here. If the photographer has nothing to license, the credit is a courtesy; if he
does, the credit is the entire price and Pigment pays it on the page. The site's conduct is
identical either way. **That is why the flat-reproduction question, which looks like the
deep one, selects no option for A or B.**

## 2. Moral rights, and Decision C seen through them

France's moral right is perpetual and sits with the heirs. Turkey's is statutory and its
post-mortem exercise bounded — **except that FSEK Article 19's final paragraph is reported
to let the Ministry of Culture exercise the Article 14/3, 15/3 and 16/3 rights in its own
name where the work matters to the country's culture, once the listed persons are gone or
the periods have run.** **[fetched: search summaries of art. 19 and of an
İstanbul Hukuk Mecmuası study of it; the statute itself would not retrieve. Do not quote
this paragraph without checking it.]** A long-lived moral-rights route, held by the state
rather than an estate.

Applied to the 61 covers: **a moral right is a right in a work, and E-001 established that
no artwork pixels enter these covers** — on that alone neither country's moral right has an
object. The branch needing no reproduction is paternity's converse: an artist's interest in
*not* having attributed to him a work he did not make. French sources describe the
paternity right being used that way against fraudulent attribution, and commentators
calling that a diversion of its purpose. **[fetched: search summaries; no case read.]**

One repository fact matters here, measured today. `coverLabel` (`js/app.js:848`) produces
**an aria-label** — "Generative cover for *Las Meninas (series) by Pablo Picasso*, painted
in the browser" — and the visible disclaimer, "a seeded Pigment interpretation", is a
**sibling `<span class="map-hint">`** (`js/app.js:2205`). **Nothing is painted into the
canvas.** The artist's name and the disclaimer are both DOM strings around an image
carrying neither, so a screenshotted cover travels with no label at all; the prerendered
stub for a walled work carries no `og:image` (measured: zero), so covers do not reach link
previews. Whether a disclosure living outside the image does its work is the same question
in both countries. **Who can raise it is not.** France: organised estates and ADAGP,
perpetually. Turkey: successors under art. 19 and a Ministry whose remit is Turkey's own
culture — and **none of the 15 artists behind the 61 records is Turkish** (measured:
Picasso 10, Kahlo 9, Dalí 8, Pollock 8, Rothko 8, Warhol 8, Matisse 2, eight singles).

## 4. Term arithmetic — the convention holds under both

Turkey: **life + 70, running from 1 January of the year following death** — the counting
France uses. **[fetched: WIPO Lex, Law No. 5846.]** And France's wartime extensions are
smaller than the primer says: **the Cour de cassation held on 27 February 2007
(n° 04-12.138) that the harmonised 70-year term absorbs them**, except where a longer term
was already running on 1 July 1995; a musical-works carve-out survives and the 30-year
"mort pour la France" supplement (CPI L123-10) was left unresolved. **[fetched: Squire Patton
Boggs commentary; the judgment itself unread.]** For a roster of painters that leaves the
1955 convention doing identical arithmetic in both countries. Thirteen artists sit in the
1946–1955 band (measured) — Léger 1955; Matisse, Kahlo and Mihri Müşfik Hanım 1954; down to
Mehoffer 1946 — all postwar deaths, so no extension question reaches the band. The
convention stays what `tools/fetch_artworks.py` says: a rule for which artists to attempt.

## 5. The 61 walled artists — and a correction I owe the record

place it and D-005 repeated that as "the unverifiable GESAM". **I was wrong, and the reason
