Philip Guston

1913–1980 · He gave up abstraction to paint hoods

Born in Montreal to Jewish refugees from Odessa, raised in Los Angeles, he found his father's body after his suicide at the age of ten and began drawing alone in a closet.

He painted anti-Klan murals in the 1930s, won every prize American art could give a muralist, then spent the 1950s as the most lyrical of the Abstract Expressionists — hovering pinks and greys that critics called abstract impressionism.
