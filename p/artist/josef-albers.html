Josef Albers

1888–1976 · One square inside another, two thousand times

A Westphalian house-painter's son and a schoolteacher by training, he arrived at the Bauhaus in 1920 as its oldest student and stayed to run the glass workshop and the preliminary course.

From 1950 until his death he painted one motif: three or four flat squares nested inside each other, in a strictly fixed format, with the paint applied straight from the tube with a palette knife and never mixed.
