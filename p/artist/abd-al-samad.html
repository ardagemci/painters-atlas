Abd al-Samad

c. 1500–c. 1595 · He carried Persian painting to India, then ran the workshop that changed it

From Shiraz, trained in the Safavid royal atelier — the tradition Behzād had led — and recruited to the Mughal court by the exiled emperor Humayun, whom he taught to draw.

From around 1572 he headed Akbar's imperial workshop, and it was under his direction that the Mughal style arrived at itself: Persian colour and page design, Indian faces and appetite for the observed world, and modelling learned from European engravings the Jesuits brought.
