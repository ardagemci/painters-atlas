Presentation in the Temple

Ambrogio Lorenzetti · 1342 · Gallerie degli Uffizi, Florence

A building, painted from inside, with a floor that actually goes somewhere. Lorenzetti tiles the pavement and runs the columns back into depth a full lifetime before perspective was codified, and he does it by eye. The ceremony is quiet — an old man holds the child, a priest waits, the mother watches — and the architecture does the arguing. Six years later the Black Death killed him, and Siena stopped painting like this.

  • The tiled floor recedes correctly, decades before the rules existed
  • The scene is set inside a building that behaves like one
  • He died in the plague of 1348, and this line of painting stopped
