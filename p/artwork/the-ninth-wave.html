The Ninth Wave

Ivan Aivazovsky · 1850 · State Russian Museum, St Petersburg · 221 × 332 cm

Survivors of a wreck cling to a mast in the trough before the ninth wave, which sailors held to be the largest of a set. The sun is coming up straight into your eyes through the water, and Aivazovsky painted it in translucent glazes so the light seems to be behind the canvas rather than on it. Three and a third metres wide. He painted the sea some six thousand times and this is the one everyone means.

  • The sun is glazed, not painted — the light comes from behind
  • They cling to a mast; the ship has already gone
  • The ninth wave was sailors' lore: the biggest of any set
