Pine Trees

Hasegawa Tōhaku · c. 1595 · Tokyo National Museum, Tokyo · Each screen 156.8 × 356 cm

A pair of six-panel screens with pines standing in fog, and the fog is unpainted paper. Tōhaku left more than half the surface empty and let the trunks fade in and out of it as if you were walking past them. Nothing is described that does not need to be. It was designated a National Treasure in 1952 and is the picture most often named when Japanese painting is asked to explain itself.

  • The mist is bare paper — most of the screen is empty
  • A pair of screens; the trees continue across the gap
  • Ink only. No gold, in the age of gold screens
