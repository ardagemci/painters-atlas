The City

Fernand Léger · 1919 · Philadelphia Museum of Art, Philadelphia · 231.1 × 298.4 cm

Léger came out of the trenches in 1917 having spent the war among machinery and men who were not artists, and painted the city as it actually arrives: signage, scaffolding, a staircase, two stencilled figures, a fragment of a letter. Nothing is behind anything else. It is three metres wide and flat as a poster, which is the point — this is the first painting that looks like graphic design, twenty years before there was any.

  • Stencilled letters, scaffolding and poles, all on one plane
  • The two figures are the only curves in it
  • Painted straight out of the war, in which he served at Verdun
