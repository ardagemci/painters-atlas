Inwangjesaekdo

Jeong Seon · 1751 · Leeum Museum of Art, Seoul

Inwangsan after rain, seen from below, with the granite dome soaked black and mist still lying in the valley. Korean painters had spent centuries inventing Chinese mountains they had never visited; Jeong Seon went out and looked at his own, and the movement got a name for it — true-view landscape. He was seventy-five. The wet rock is laid on in broad sweeps, the pines in dots, and it is unmistakably one specific hill.

  • The granite is soaked, not shaded — laid on in broad wet sweeps
  • True-view landscape: a real Korean mountain, not an imagined Chinese one
  • Painted at seventy-five, of the hill he could see from home
