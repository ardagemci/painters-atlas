Et in Arcadia ego

Nicolas Poussin · 1637–1638 · Musée du Louvre, Paris · 85 × 121 cm

Three shepherds and a woman have found a tomb in paradise and are reading what is cut into it: even in Arcadia, I am here. One of them traces the letters with a finger, and his own shadow falls across the stone as he does it — the shadow of a living arm lying on the word for death. Poussin painted the subject twice. This is the later, quieter one, in which nobody panics. It is a picture of comprehension arriving.

  • A shepherd's shadow falls across the inscription he reads
  • Nobody is frightened; they are working out the Latin
  • Poussin painted this subject twice, ten years apart
