The Joy of Life

Henri Matisse · 1905–06 · Barnes Foundation, Philadelphia

An Arcadia with the rules removed: bodies drawn in one sinuous line, colour cut loose from description, each figure keeping its own scale as though the picture were assembled out of separate dreams. Signac, who had backed him, called it a catastrophe. Leo Stein bought it and hung it near his Picassos, setting off the rivalry that produced Les Demoiselles d'Avignon a year later. The ring of dancers at the centre would become, in 1910, The Dance.

  • The small ring of dancers at the back — the seed of The Dance
  • Every figure is scaled to itself; nothing shares a horizon
  • Line does the drawing, colour does the mood, and they never quite agree
