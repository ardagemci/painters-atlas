Self-Portrait in a Straw Hat

Élisabeth Vigée Le Brun · 1782 · The National Gallery, London

She saw a Rubens in Antwerp with a hat throwing a shadow across the face and went home determined to do it in daylight, outdoors, on herself. The result made her reputation: palette in hand, looking straight out, entirely at ease. Within a few years she was Marie Antoinette's painter, and within a few more she was crossing Europe ahead of the Revolution, working in every capital that would have her.

  • The idea came from a Rubens she saw in Antwerp two years earlier
  • Painted in open daylight, which is why the shadow behaves
  • She holds her palette: this is a working portrait, not a society one
