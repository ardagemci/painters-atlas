The Jewish Cemetery

Jacob van Ruisdael · c. 1654–55 · Detroit Institute of Arts, Detroit

The tombs are real, drawn at the Portuguese Jewish cemetery outside Amsterdam. Everything around them is invented: a ruined church, a dead beech, a stream cutting through, a storm clearing to let a rainbow over the whole arrangement. Ruisdael has built an argument rather than a view — stone outlasts us, trees fall, weather passes — and made it without a single figure to say so. Goethe wrote an essay about it.

  • The tombs were drawn from life; the ruin and the stream were not
  • A rainbow arrives on the right as the storm pulls away
  • Not one human figure anywhere in it
