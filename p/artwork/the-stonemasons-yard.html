The Stonemason's Yard

Canaletto · c. 1725 · The National Gallery, London

Before Canaletto learned to sell Venice to tourists, he painted this: a temporary yard full of building stone, washing strung across a window, a child who has fallen over and a mother running, a woman spinning on a balcony. The campanile leans slightly because it did. It is the least glamorous picture he ever made and by common consent the best, and he was about twenty-eight.

  • A working yard, not a view — the stone is for the church opposite
  • Bottom left, a child has fallen; the mother is already moving
  • Laundry, a spinning wheel, a cracked wall: nothing tidied for sale
