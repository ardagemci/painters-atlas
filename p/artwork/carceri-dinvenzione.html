Giovanni Battista Piranesi · 1750/1761 · The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

Imaginary prisons: vaults the size of cathedrals, staircases that arrive nowhere, winches and beams and chains serving no purpose anyone can name. Piranesi etched the first set in his twenties, then went back eleven years later and bit the plates deeper until the dark closed in. There are no prisoners to speak of. The architecture is the sentence, and every later drawing of an impossible building owes it something.

  • The stairs connect to nothing; follow one and it contradicts itself
  • The 1761 reworking darkened the plates and made them far worse
  • Almost no prisoners — the building itself is the punishment
