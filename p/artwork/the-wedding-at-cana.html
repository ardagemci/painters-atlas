The Wedding at Cana

Paolo Veronese · 1563 · Musée du Louvre, Paris

Ten metres of wedding party, about a hundred and thirty guests, and Christ sitting dead centre being entirely ignored by nearly all of them. Veronese painted it for a monastery refectory in Venice, so the monks ate their supper facing a better one. Napoleon's army took it in 1797 and cut it in half to move it. It now hangs in the Louvre on the wall opposite the Mona Lisa, watching the crowd watch her.

  • Christ is at the centre and almost nobody at the table notices
  • The musicians in the foreground are portraits of Venetian painters
  • Cut in two by Napoleon's troops to get it out of Venice
