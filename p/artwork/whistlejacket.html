Whistlejacket

George Stubbs · c. 1762 · The National Gallery, London

A racehorse at life size, rearing, against nothing at all — no rider, no groom, no landscape, no sky. Just warm ground colour and an animal painted with the accuracy of a man who had spent eighteen months in a Lincolnshire farmhouse dissecting horses and drawing what was underneath. The emptiness was probably the patron's decision and it makes the picture modern. He is simply, enormously, there.

  • No background whatsoever — the field of colour is the whole setting
  • Stubbs dissected horses for eighteen months before painting them
  • Life-size: the canvas is nearly three metres tall
