At the Moulin Rouge

Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec · 1892–1895 · The Art Institute of Chicago, Chicago · 123 × 141 cm

A wooden balustrade cuts the whole foreground on the diagonal and holds you outside the room. Behind it five of Lautrec's circle sit round a table. The face that stops you is at the right edge, half cut off by it: the singer May Milton, lit from beneath in a shocking acid green. The Moulin Rouge was built for electric light, and Lautrec painted what electric light does to a face.

  • May Milton's face is acid green, lit from beneath
  • Lautrec is at the back, beside his much taller cousin
  • He kept a permanently reserved table in this room
