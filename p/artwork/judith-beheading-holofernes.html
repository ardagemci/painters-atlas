Judith Beheading Holofernes

Caravaggio · c. 1599–1602 · Palazzo Barberini (Gallerie Nazionali), Rome · 145 × 195 cm

Caravaggio paints the cut itself: the blade already in the neck, three jets of blood, and Judith leaning back from her own work with an expression nearer distaste than triumph. Beside her an old servant waits with a cloth, and it is the servant's face, not Judith's, that has fully decided. It is the least heroic beheading in painting, and being unheroic is what it was for.

  • The blade is mid-cut and she is leaning away from it
  • The servant's face has decided; Judith's has not
  • Three jets of blood, and no decorous trickle
