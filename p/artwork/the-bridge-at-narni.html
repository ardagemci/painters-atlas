The Bridge at Narni

Camille Corot · 1826 · Musée du Louvre, Paris · 34 × 48 cm

A broken Roman bridge over the Nera, painted outdoors in one go on a small panel, in the hard Italian light of a single afternoon. Corot then went back to the studio and produced the large, correct, tree-framed Salon version — and it is this thirty-four-centimetre sketch that history kept. Everything Impressionism would later insist on is already here: the whole thing decided by where the light falls, and finished before it moved.

  • Painted on the spot, at a size you could carry home
  • The Salon version he made from it is politer and duller
  • The bridge is Roman, and had already been broken for centuries
