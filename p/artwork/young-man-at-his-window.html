Young Man at His Window

Gustave Caillebotte · 1876 · The Getty Center, Los Angeles · 117 × 82 cm

René Caillebotte stands at the window of the family apartment on the rue de Miromesnil, hands in his pockets, watching a woman cross the boulevard below. He is the painter’s brother, and he blocks the view we came for. Caillebotte gives the street an engineer’s exactness and gives the man nothing but a dark silhouette. Zola, seeing it at the 1876 Impressionist exhibition, called that exactness “anti-artistic”. The complaint was the point. The picture is about the act of looking, and it puts you behind someone who will not turn round.
