Colorful Realm of Living Beings

Itō Jakuchū · c. 1757–66 · Museum of the Imperial Collections, Tokyo

Thirty silk scrolls of birds, fish, insects and flowers, made over roughly a decade by a Kyoto greengrocer who handed the family business to his brother at forty so he could paint full time. He kept live roosters in the garden and drew them until he knew them cold. The pigments are laid on the back of the silk as well as the front, so the colour glows through. This scroll is the lotus pond.

  • One scroll of thirty; the set was conceived and painted together
  • Colour is applied behind the silk as well as in front, to glow through
  • He left the grocery trade at forty and kept roosters as models
