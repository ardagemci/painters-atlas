Haboku Sansui (Splashed-Ink Landscape)

Sesshū Tōyō · 1495 · Tokyo National Museum, Tokyo · 148.6 × 32.7 cm

A cliff, a hut, a boat and two small figures — and above them the ink simply stops describing anything, just fast wet blots on a sheet a metre and a half tall. Haboku is the most extreme manner the tradition had, and Sesshū took it to the edge in 1495. This is about as close to non-figuration as the fifteenth century gets anywhere. Stand close: the whole landscape is a handful of strokes.

  • The top of the sheet stops describing anything
  • A cliff, a hut, a boat, two figures
  • Two sources call it a National Treasure of Japan
