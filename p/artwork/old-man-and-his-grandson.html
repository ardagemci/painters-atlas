An Old Man and His Grandson

Domenico Ghirlandaio · c. 1490 · Musée du Louvre, Paris

The old man's nose is deformed by disease and Ghirlandaio paints it exactly, lumps and all, then paints the child looking up at him with complete trust. Neither fact cancels the other, which is the whole achievement. A window at the back opens onto a winding road, the way Flemish portraits did it. Nobody knows who they were. It is one of the few Renaissance portraits where affection, rather than status, is the subject.

  • The nose is rhinophyma, recorded without softening
  • The child's hand rests on his chest; he is not flinching
  • The window and road are borrowed from Netherlandish portraiture
