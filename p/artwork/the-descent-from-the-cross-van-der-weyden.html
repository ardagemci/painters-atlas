The Descent from the Cross

Rogier van der Weyden · c. 1435–1438 · Museo del Prado, Madrid · 204.5 × 261.5 cm

Ten figures at life size, packed into a box barely deeper than a carved shrine — there is nowhere for any of them to go. The Virgin has collapsed, and her body is drawn as a near-exact rhyme of her son's: same curve, same fall, one directly above the other. That is the invention. The grief is not performed by the faces; it is built into the composition.

  • The Virgin's body rhymes with Christ's, curve for curve
  • Ten life-size figures in a shrine-shallow box
  • Grief at full pitch, and nothing is moving
